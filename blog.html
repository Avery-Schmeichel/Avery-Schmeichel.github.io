The site I chose to observe is the Grand Bazaar in Istanbul, Turkey. Initial observations of the Grand Bazar: to get to the bazaar, you must walk on down hill streets. It almost felt like the building was in a valley or underground. There are many entrances and exits for the building. The entrances were arched walkways, labeled with the name “Grand Bazaar”. You must go through a security scanner at the entrance. It is a very crowded space, people everywhere, many shoppers and many sellers. It was confusing to navigate and a place that you could easily get lost. The shops at the Grand Bazaar were of various kinds but the most frequent included: jewelry, tea, Turkish delights , purses/bags, sport jerseys, glassware, clothing & various other trinkets! The Bazaar also organized the goods into different halls, there were jewelry halls, antique halls, rug halls, lamp halls, etc.

I sat down to have tea at a lovely tea shop and enjoyed a cup of warm apple tea. The owner immediately offered many treats and other drinks to go with my apple tea. I politely declined, but it was interesting to see the haggling even at the tea cafes. While I sat at the tea cafe and observed the bazaar around me. The first thing I noticed was the different demeanors of the shop owners. I saw some shop owners sleeping in their shop, others patiently waiting for tourists to enter their shop and there were many passionate shop owners standing in the hall of the bazaar trying to entice shoppers with sales, deals or bargains. As far as the customers in the bazaar, it seemed that it was mostly made up of tourists, although there did seem to be many men on phones rushing around as well. It was also interesting to observe the customer's demeanor while shopping. Some customers didn't question the prices at all and others haggled lots until they got a price they were satisfied with.

Another observation is the Haggeling part of the bazaar, customers will try to get prices down, and sellers will try to get the prices up and make the buyer buy more items. The hours for the Grand Bazaar is 8:30am - 7:00pm Monday- Saturday and closed on Sundays , So as far as the Grand Bazaar being a public space, it is a public space during operating hours. Anyone can walk into the bazaar, there is no obligation to buy something, although you may be persuaded. There were many tea shops and Turkish delight shops with seating for customers, but there were also some public seating that were not attached to shops. The seating options were random: I saw a desk chair, an office chair, foldable chairs and wooden chairs. There were also public bathrooms, with no cost as well as wudu washing stations prior to prayer.

The relationship between public and private regarding the bazaar would be that the grand bazaar is public, during their open hours of the day/week. Also certain areas of the bazaar would be considered private, like the tea cafes. In the tea cafes you would have to buy something to sit at their tables/ chairs. The grand bazaar is comparable to the local West Acres mall that we discussed in class earlier the semester. There is a mix of public and private spaces in one larger space. The bazaar could also be comparable to the Roman Forum. When the Roman Forum was first created it was a market place and then later in time it shifted to be a more public space for public affairs. This shows how throughout much of human history there have been public places for markets and shopping. Public places where you can get multiple goods at once, or even just walk around with friends and enjoy the atmosphere. Even if we look back on Pompeii, on a much smaller scale they had the snack bars, for individuals to buy items quickly and all in one place.

The Grand Bazaar has a unique vibe/atmosphere that I have never experienced anywhere else. It was a cool historical and cultural experience to have in Turkey. The grand bazaar connects to market spaces of the past and also connects to the market places of today.

On the western edge of the city of Paris, in the 16th arrondissement, you will find a park named the Bois de Boulogne. The Bois is the most celebrated park of the Second Empire. The Bois is home to beautiful scenery, walking paths, vendors, and cafés. There are also activities and events hosted in the Bois de Boulogne such as ice-skating, picnicking, horse racing at Long-champ Racecourse and so much more.

The impressionist painters of the late 1880s captured the true beauty of the Bois de Boulogne. In this field guide essay, I will describe how Haussmannization created the Bois, how social leisure norms affected the use of the Bois, and how the impressionists captured all the beauty of the Bois.

In Paris, nineteenth century, the city was experiencing an industrial revolution, which led to population growth, overcrowding and consequently the spreading of diseases. Louis Napoleon III ( President of France from 1848 to 1852, then Emperor 1852-1870) was set on bringing order and structure to Paris. Baron George-Eugène Haussmann, appointed by Napoleon came into office in 1853, as the lead of the project for redesigning Paris. Haussmann was influential to the city of Paris as he was in charge of developing Paris into a modern city post-industrial revolution.

Many people talk about the negative outcomes due to Huassmannization, some examples of these outcomes included Haussmann’s mismanagement of money, rise of rent in Paris and some Parisians deceived about the money and time aspects of the redesigning of Paris. I believe Haussmann had a lot of positive effects on the city of Paris. Some of the positive contributions include overall improved health of Parisians. Public health was improved greatly by fixing the sewer system, widening the streets, creating more housing, lowering overcrowding which lessened the chance of disease. Sharon Marcus wrote an excerpt of the visual effects of the widening of the streets “By focusing the pedestrian’s gaze on what lay at the end of the street, perspectival organization placed the apartments that lined streets in a peripheral space that dissolved on the edge of vision.” (Marcus, p.728). Marcus was able to describe how the streets appeared in Paris post-Haussmannization.

The positive contributions of Baron Haussmann’s redesigning of Paris that I will highlight is the creation of the Bois de Boulogne. In the Impressionism book by Robert L. Herbert, he gives this statistic: “In 1848 there had only been 47 acres of municipal parks in Paris…totaling by 1870, not 50 acres but 4,500” (Herbert,p.142). Herberts statistic displayed a positive side of Haussmann’s work in Paris. Haussmann appointed Adolphe Alphand as the lead of park services for the redesigning of Paris. Alphand’s chief gardener was Pierre Barillet-Deschamps. Together, Alphand and Barillet-Deschamps were able to create the Bois de Boulogne. They created artificial lakes, waterfalls, rock formations, and walking paths just in time for the Universal Exposition of 1867. The park became a popular excursion from the center of Paris. Before long, the Bois became known as the “privileged pomade of Paris”, at the time the park was enjoyed by upper-class Parisians. Guidebooks at the time recommended the Bois as an attraction as foreigners wouldn’t be “distracted” at the sight of the working class.

The Bois de Boulogne is a great example of Parisians' love for leisure in the late nineteenth century. Whether Parisians wanted to ice-skate, eat at cafés, picnic, go on walks or bike rides, go boating, or watch horse racing at long champ, the Bois de Boulogne was the place to be. The Impressionist painters of the time witnessed this phenomenon and made some excellent paintings that depict the Bois de Boulogne and its effect on Parisians. In Renoir’s Skaters in the Bois de Boulogne (1868), he depicts a landscape painting of many people ice skating on a lake at the Bois de Boulogne. In the painting, you can tell a lot about the Bois. You can see the vast nature in the park with the trees at the horizon line and the trees in the left foreground of the painting. You also see many Parisians enjoying the activity of ice skating whilst taking in the wintery scenery that the Bois de Boulogne has to offer. There are also vendors in the foreground of the painting, which is an accurate depiction of this time. Vendors, cafés, and restaurants were popular places of leisure for Parisians.

Another aspect of leisure for the visitors of the Bois de Boulogne was the horse races at the long-champ course. One impressionist painter in particular took a liking to painting scenes at the long-champ races. That was Edgar Degas, Degas painted countless scenes at the long-champ race course. One painting captures the essence of the long-champ races. Degas’s painting titled The False Start (1870). In the painting, the viewer gets an up-close view of a horse and a rider. The background shows the grandstands at the long-champ racecourse as well as some trees and natural aspects of the Bois.

The Bois de Boulogne was one of Baron Haussmann’s positive outcomes of his redesigning of the Paris project. Haussmann, Alphand, and Barillet-Deschamps worked together to create a place of beauty and leisure for the people of Paris. Parisians at the time enjoyed a wide range of outdoor activities that the Bois had to offer. The impressionist painters of the time saw this beautiful park and created magnificent paintings that represented the Parisian society in the late nineteenth century.

Art Therapy as Treatment for Major Depressive Disorder Avery Schmeichel PSY 324 Research Project Intro Art therapy is a relatively new form of psychotherapy. Art therapy uses art and creative expression to help further treat mental illnesses and improve an individual's emotional and cognitive well being. Art therapy can be used for many mental illnesses, including Major Depressive Disorder. There are multiple studies that have been conducted on using art therapy as treatment for Major Depressive Disorder. The National Institute of Health did a study on using art psychotherapy as an add-on treatment to individuals already undergoing pharmacotherapy in South Korea. The scope of this essay is examining the positive effects of art psychotherapy and a brief history of art therapy as well. What is Art Therapy? What is art therapy? The American Art Therapy Association defines art therapy as “ a mental health profession that enriches the lives of individuals, families and communities through active art making, creative process, applied psychological theory, and a human experience within a psychotherapeutic relationship.” (American Art therapy Association, 2025) The Art Therapy Credentials Board defines art therapy as “Art therapy uses art media, the creative process, and the resulting artwork as a therapeutic and healing process.” (Art therapy credentials board, 2025). The term art therapy can be defined in many ways, because art psychotherapy can look and be experienced in many different ways. The main principle of art therapy is that art can heal and help benefit patients emotionally and cognitively. Art therapy can include verbal talk therapy or can be completely non verbal. History of Art therapy The name ‘art therapy’ was created in 1942 by british artist Adrian Hill. Adrian Hill is considered by many art therapy professionals to be the founder of the field. . The origin of art therapy dates back to 1938 when Hill got diagnosed with tuberculosis and used art to pass the time while in the hospital. Hill was able to help his boredom and express emotions of discontentment to pass the time in the hospital by creating art. By 1942 Hill began to teach art to a small group of patients recovering from tuberculosis. People started to notice that tuberculosis patients that were doing art and being creative started to suffer less symptoms. Not long after, Hill began an official art therapy program at the hospital. In 1945, Hill wrote the book “Art vs Illness; a story of art therapy” which is thought to be the start of the field, with a tangible text. In the 1960s is when the first art therapy degree programs began, starting with Drexel University and later followed by New York University. What do Art Therapists do? Art therapists are clinicians with masters degrees or higher who work with a wide variety of individuals and/or groups. Art therapists use art to treat a variety of mental health illnesses or emotional issues, to hopefully provide healing. Art is a therapeutic tool to improve the cognitive and emotional well being of patients. Art provides a unique way to communicate and express emotions nonverbally. The actual activities of art therapy can vary as well, some examples of art therapy activities include: painting or drawing your emotions to better understand and recognize them, making a sculpture of your family to explore family dynamics, and making a homemade journal to later write about your experiences or emotions in. The activities are truly endless for art therapy. What is Major Depressive Disorder? Our textbook defines Major Depressive Disorder as “A disorder in which the individual experiences acute but time-limited episodes of depressive symptoms.” ( Whitebourne, 2023) Some of the common symptoms of major depressive disorder are persistent episodes of depressed mood, insomnia or hypersomnia, gaining or losing weight, fatigue, loss of interest in once enjoyable activities, feelings of guilt or worthlessness and physical symptoms like headaches or digestive issues. Traditional Treatments for Major Depressive Disorder Traditional treatments for major depressive disorder include psychotherapy, medication and lifestyle changes. Psychotherapy is one of the most common treatments for major depressive disorders. Some examples of psychotherapy treatments for major depressive disorder are: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), CBT could help identify negative thought patterns the client may have. There are many other variations of CBT available for depression as well. The most common medications for treatment for major depressive disorder are Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) (ex. Zoloft and Lexapro) and serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) (ex. Effexor and Fetzima) these medications can decrease depressive symptoms and improve mood. SSRIs work by blocking the reuptake process in the brain, therefore having more serotonin available. It is important to note that these drugs can have many side effects. Some of the common side effects are weight gain or loss, sleep disturbances and nausea. Changes in lifestyle can also be used to treat Major Depressive Disorder. Things like regular exercise, healthy diet, good sleep hygiene, stress management and social activities. These lifestyle changes can significantly improve symptoms of MDD but they take time and consistency. Some individuals may need CBT or medications as well as lifestyle adjustments to improve their MDD. Art therapy as treatment for depression Now we will be examining Art therapy as treatment for depression. The National Institute of Health has an article on a study that examined the effects of art psychotherapy on individuals with moderate to severe major depressive disorder that are already using SSRIs as treatment. In this study 42 Major Depressive Disorder patients were recruited from an outpatient psychiatric facility in Seoul, South Korea. Participants were assigned a random, open label basis to an experimental group where they were treated with art psychotherapy and pharmacotherapy or the control group that was treated with pharmacotherapy alone. The pre and post tests were based on the hamilton depression ranking scale, beck depression inventory and remission rates were recorded. The study was 6 weeks in length. The results of the study showed that the experimental group showed better improvement compared to the group that got only pharmacotherapy alone. The art psychotherapy was one fifty minute session a week. Each session was structured activities. The first session included drawing your feelings, and the processes and procedures of art psychotherapy were explained. In session two they did free drawing, and unconscious thoughts were shown through free association. Session three was drawing self portraits, the purpose was to explore how ego identity was affected by major depressive episodes. In the 4th session, there was an interpersonal inventory for participants to grasp the social support and interpersonal relationship issues. In session 5 the participants did family drawings to get a closer look at family relationships and their effect on the depression. The 6th and final session included landscape drawings to view depression with a broader scope. The conclusion of the study showed that the individuals in the experimental group had a significant decrease in their depressive symptoms. The study also acknowledged that more rigorous research is needed in the art therapy field to create and facilitate a better understanding of the effects of art therapy. There is a lot still unknown and undiscovered about the field of art therapy, this study shows the need for more research as there was a positive outcome for the experimental group. Analyze NIH study on art therapy in addition to psychotherapy The study done by the National Institute of Health was interesting because it was testing the effectiveness of art therapy, for patients that were already undergoing treatment for Major Depressive Disorder. Some of the limitations mentioned about the study included that the research could have been more rigorous, as that it was a combined study, with art psychotherapy and pharmacotherapy there are multiple factors of treatment happening. The study also only examined the positive effects of add-on treatment, and no mention of the possible negatives of add-on treatment. Conclusion In conclusion, Art therapy is a unique and relatively new form of psychotherapy. There are so many forms of art therapy and it can look many different ways. There are countless activities and so many that are yet to be done yet in the field of art therapy. The history of art therapy goes back to a tuberculosis outbreak in 1938, and now in the United States there are over 8,000 credentialed art therapists. The national institute of health study examined shows that art therapy alongside pharmacotherapy can be beneficial for individuals decreasing their depressive symptoms.
