How the Bois de Boulogne Affected Paris and Impressionist Art in the Nineteenth Century On the western edge of the city of Paris, in the 16th arrondissement, you will find a park named the Bois de Boulogne. The Bois is the most celebrated park of the Second Empire. The Bois is home to beautiful scenery, walking paths, vendors, and cafés. There are also activities and events hosted in the Bois de Boulogne such as ice-skating, picnicking, horse racing at Long-champ Racecourse and so much more. The impressionist painters of the late 1880s captured the true beauty of the Bois de Boulogne. In this field guide essay, I will describe how Haussmannization created the Bois, how social leisure norms affected the use of the Bois, and how the impressionists captured all the beauty of the Bois.  In Paris, nineteenth century, the city was experiencing an industrial revolution, which led to population growth, overcrowding and consequently the spreading of diseases. Louis Napoleon III ( President of France from 1848 to 1852, then Emperor 1852-1870) was set on bringing order and structure to Paris. Baron George-Eugène Haussmann, appointed by Napoleon came into office in 1853, as the lead of the project for redesigning Paris. Haussmann was influential to the city of Paris as he was in charge of developing Paris into a modern city post-industrial revolution.  Many people talk about the negative outcomes due to Huassmannization, some examples of these outcomes included Haussmann’s mismanagement of money, rise of rent in Paris and some Parisians deceived about the money and time aspects of the redesigning of Paris. I believe Haussmann had a lot of positive effects on the city of Paris. Some of the positive contributions include overall improved health of Parisians. Public health was improved greatly by fixing the sewer system, widening the streets, creating more housing, lowering overcrowding which lessened the chance of disease. Sharon Marcus wrote an excerpt of the visual effects of the widening of the streets “By focusing the pedestrian’s gaze on what lay at the end of the street, perspectival organization placed the apartments that lined streets in a peripheral space that dissolved on the edge of vision.” (Marcus, p.728). Marcus was able to describe how the streets appeared in Paris post-Haussmannization.  The positive contributions of Baron Haussmann’s redesigning of Paris that I will highlight is the creation of the Bois de Boulogne. In the Impressionism book by Robert L. Herbert, he gives this statistic: “In 1848 there had only been 47 acres of municipal parks in Paris…totaling by 1870, not 50 acres but 4,500” (Herbert,p.142). Herberts statistic displayed a positive side of Haussmann’s work in Paris. Haussmann appointed Adolphe Alphand as the lead of park services for the redesigning of Paris. Alphand’s chief gardener was Pierre Barillet-Deschamps. Together, Alphand and Barillet-Deschamps were able to create the Bois de Boulogne. They created artificial lakes, waterfalls, rock formations, and walking paths just in time for the Universal Exposition of 1867. The park became a popular excursion from the center of Paris. Before long, the Bois became known as the “privileged pomade of Paris”, at the time the park was enjoyed by upper-class Parisians. Guidebooks at the time recommended the Bois as an attraction as foreigners wouldn’t be “distracted” at the sight of the working class.  The Bois de Boulogne is a great example of Parisians' love for leisure in the late nineteenth century. Whether Parisians wanted to ice-skate, eat at cafés, picnic, go on walks or bike rides, go boating, or watch horse racing at long champ, the Bois de Boulogne was the place to be. The Impressionist painters of the time witnessed this phenomenon and made some excellent paintings that depict the Bois de Boulogne and its effect on Parisians. In Renoir’s Skaters in the Bois de Boulogne (1868), he depicts a landscape painting of many people ice skating on a lake at the Bois de Boulogne. In the painting, you can tell a lot about the Bois. You can see the vast nature in the park with the trees at the horizon line and the trees in the left foreground of the painting. You also see many Parisians enjoying the activity of ice skating whilst taking in the wintery scenery that the Bois de Boulogne has to offer. There are also vendors in the foreground of the painting, which is an accurate depiction of this time. Vendors, cafés, and restaurants were popular places of leisure for Parisians.  Another aspect of leisure for the visitors of the Bois de Boulogne was the horse races at the long-champ course. One impressionist painter in particular took a liking to painting scenes at the long-champ races. That was Edgar Degas, Degas painted countless scenes at the long-champ race course. One painting captures the essence of the long-champ races. Degas’s painting titled The False Start (1870). In the painting, the viewer gets an up-close view of a horse and a rider. The background shows the grandstands at the long-champ racecourse as well as some trees and natural aspects of the Bois. The Bois de Boulogne was one of Baron Haussmann’s positive outcomes of his redesigning of the Paris project. Haussmann, Alphand, and Barillet-Deschamps worked together to create a place of beauty and leisure for the people of Paris. Parisians at the time enjoyed a wide range of outdoor activities that the Bois had to offer. The impressionist painters of the time saw this beautiful park and created magnificent paintings that represented the Parisian society in the late nineteenth century.  Bibliography Herbert, Robert.
